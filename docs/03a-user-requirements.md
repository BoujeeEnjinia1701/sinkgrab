# SinkGrab user requirements

What well-digging crews, WASH engineers, well committees and fabricators need, written from their side. They are drawn from the users and stakeholders in SKG-PRB-001 (Tables 1 and 2) and its operating environment, and they have not yet been validated with a crew; co-design sessions with the first partner will revise them. Product requirements that meet these needs are in SKG-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix SKG-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). Method: STANDARDS section 22.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Dig below the water table | Deepen a flooded well 2 to 3 m below the dry-season water table without pumping it out | High | Well-digging crews; well committees (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-002 | Safety | Nobody in the well | Do all the digging and undercutting from the surface, so nobody works under water or in bad air | High | Well-digging crews; WASH engineers |
| UR-003 | Performance | Keep the lining straight | Sink the caisson rings evenly, see tilt early and correct it | High | Well-digging crews; WASH engineers |
| UR-004 | Deployment and assembly | Fits the wells we dig | Work inside the caisson ring sizes in use, about 0.8 to 1.3 m inside diameter | High | Well-digging crews (PRB operating environment) |
| UR-005 | Performance | Enough soil a day | Lift enough soil each cycle to finish the wet part of a well in a few working days | High | Well-digging crews; well committees |
| UR-006 | Usability | Effort a crew can keep up | Crank, turn and handle with forces a crew of three to five can keep up all day | High | Well-digging crews |
| UR-007 | Safety | Lifting gear we can trust | Never load the tripod past its rating, whatever the crew does; lifting parts proof-tested before use | High | WASH engineers; owners of the HatchSide tripod (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-008 | Logistics | Portable | Carry every piece by one or two people and move the whole kit in a small pickup | Medium | Well-digging crews; WASH engineers |
| UR-009 | Maintenance | Built and mended locally | Build and repair it from drawings with a stick welder, drill and hand tools, from steel the local stockholder sells | High | Local fabricators |
| UR-010 | Cost | Affordable | Cost a programme or a well committee can meet | Medium | Well committees; WASH engineers |
| UR-011 | Environment and durability | Works in our soils | Dig silt, fine sand, laterite, clayey sand and gravel; boulders and rock handled another way | Medium | Well-digging crews (PRB operating environment) |
| UR-012 | Safety | Safe air at the well | Know the shaft air is safe before work, after breaks and whenever someone leans over the shaft | High | Well-digging crews; WASH engineers; safety authorities |
| UR-013 | Regulatory | A method we can specify | A documented, repeatable method with stop rules, so the work can be specified, trained and inspected | Medium | WASH engineers; groundwater departments |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.

UR-011 and UR-012 had no requirement before kit 1.8; PR-013 and PR-012 now meet them (accepted by Amish on 2026-10-25, SKG-DEC-001 O3).
