# SinkGrab STEM check

**Verdict: open (two rows fail with a fix proposed, both for option B; thirteen to confirm by trial).** SinkGrab's mechanism, digging a flooded caisson well from the surface with a rope grab and an under-curb scraper on a hand capstan, sits within known science. The independent review of 2026-10-25 found two things it relied on did not hold on paper: the rings sinking 3 m without de-watering (S21) and a release window for the shear devices between the working pull and the HatchSide tripod's rating (S6, S7, E2), and the scraper's turning force was over sustained limits (B4). Amish decided the fixes on 2026-10-25 ("i approve of your recommendations", SKG-DEC-001 O2, O4, O5): a bentonite slurry in the overcut annulus is part of the sinking method; a friction slip clutch on the crank shaft and a load indicator at the dead end replace both shear devices, with proof at 1.25 times the rated load; and four people turn the scraper. Each rests on a figure only a trial can give (the lubricated friction, the clutch's setting tolerance, the toe force), so those rows are now plausible, to confirm at TRL 4. Of 48 claims for both concept options, 33 are within known science, 13 are plausible and can only be confirmed by a trial at TRL 4 (S2, S3, S5, S6, S7, S8, S10a, S14a, S21, S22, E2, B4, OB3), and two fail with a fix proposed for option B only (OB5, OB6), which waits on the concept choice (O1). `project.yaml` records `stem_check: open`. Six claims failed when first checked on the detailed model and each is now fixed in the design under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 ("when a STEM check row fails, propose and implement the best fix for the design and its constructability"): the shear link's calibrated pin bent across an open gap (since replaced under O4), the brake's weight slackened the band, the assembled grab was lifted by two people, the full spoil tub had no safe way off the doors, the gas detector did not read carbon dioxide (SKG-DEC-001 K1 to K5), and, from the review, the gas was read only at the collar (K22). The scraper arm's eye (S20) was fixed as design for construction (K6). Method: STANDARDS section 20, fix rule. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, results in `docs/04-calcs/stem_results.csv`.

## The fixes (SKG-DEC-001 K1 to K5 and K22)

1. **Shear link (S5, K1).** The 3:1 tackle's dead end hangs on a calibrated pin of about 1.9 mm that must release between 1.20 and 1.47 kN, so the line never loads the HatchSide tripod past its 150 kg rating. As drawn in kit 1.7 the rope's thimble hung on the bare pin across the 12 mm gap between the two link plates. A pin loaded across an open gap fails in bending long before it shears: about 0.15 kN here, so the grab would have opened on every lift. A hardened 16 mm sleeve now fills the gap and carries the thimble, with a hardened bush in each plate and clearances under 0.1 mm, so the pin is cut at the two faces of the sleeve as the calculation assumes. Pins are break-tested in a spare link, not loose. About USD 3. On 2026-10-25 Amish's decision O4 replaced the shear link with a load indicator that does not release (S5 restated).
2. **Brake that is on when let go (S11, K2).** The brake lever's link pin sat 25 mm below its pivot on the well side, so the falling weight pushed the link toward the band's live end and slackened it. The lever pivot is now 67 mm above the floor on a plate on the outside of the rail, and the link runs on the band's tangent to a pin on an upright arm 60 mm above the pivot. As the weight falls the pin moves away from the band, which tightens it; the ratio is about 10.3:1 against the 10.2:1 the calculation uses. About USD 3, with the new anchor eye and posts (K13).
3. **Assembled grab (B1, K3).** At 52.7 kg (52.5 kg before the load indicator) the assembled grab is beyond two people, whose team capacity is about two thirds of their combined 50 kg, and beyond three. The build plan now assembles it on the line under the tripod and moves it only with the capstan; it travels in pieces of 25 kg or less. No cost.
4. **Spoil tub (B2, K4).** A tub holding one bite weighs about 50 kg and the kit 1.7 design did not say how it leaves the doors. Two people now slide it off the closed doors on a board to the ground and tip it at the heap, about 250 N to start it; it is never lifted full. No cost.
5. **Carbon dioxide (C2, K5).** Carbon dioxide is 1.5 times as dense as air and collects in wells. A four-gas detector reads oxygen, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulphide and flammable gas but not carbon dioxide, and it can take about 7 % carbon dioxide to pull oxygen down to the usual 19.5 % alarm, while 4 % is immediately dangerous (Henderson 2010). A portable 0 to 5 % carbon dioxide safety monitor (BOM line 35, USD 399) is now used beside the four-gas detector before work, after breaks and whenever anyone leans over the shaft; new requirement PR-012 asks for this air test. Nobody enters the well in any case.
6. **Gas read at depth (C2, K22).** The independent review found that an instrument held at the collar reads only the collar's air, while carbon dioxide pools at depth in still air. Both instruments are now lowered on a cord in a mesh bag and read at 2 m below the collar, at mid depth and 1 m above the water, each held for at least its stated response time; a reading over range counts as a fail. No cost.

## Rows decided by Amish on 2026-10-25

- **S21, sinking without de-watering (PR-001, SKG-DEC-001 O2).** The string sinks to 3 m only while skin friction stays below 1.66 kPa; the central unaided estimate (2 kPa) already stops it, and soil in full contact gives about 25 kPa. Decided: bentonite slurry fed down a pipe into the 40 mm overcut annulus is part of the method from the first trial, tried first in the test pit. No figure for the lubricated friction is claimed, so the row is plausible, to confirm at TRL 4; PR-001 is at risk.
- **S4 to S7 and E2, overload device and proof basis (PR-007, O4).** The shear link's window left 3 N to the rating, sheave friction and breakout suction made it trip or overload, and the crank pin ran above shear yield; twice the rated load exceeded the tripod's proof. Decided: a friction slip clutch on the crank shaft, set on CalRig to slip between 1,127 N (third layer) and 1,472 N (first layer) at the capstan, and a peak-reading load indicator at the dead end; proof at 1.25 times the 150 kg rated load, 188 kg. The steady full-grab pull at the capstan is 1,068 N, a margin of 1.06 on the third layer on a ±5 % setting, so S6, S7 and E2 are plausible, to confirm on CalRig; S4 passes on paper.
- **B4, turning the scraper (O5).** 142 N each for two people against about 100 N. Decided: a 2.0 m T-bar if the layout allowed it, else four people; the 2.0 m bar clears the tripod legs only below about 0.8 m above ground, so four people turn the 1.4 m bar, 71 to 84 N each; plausible, to confirm in the timed trial.

## Rows that fail, with a fix proposed

- **OB6, sinking with the bailer (option B, O1).** Option B has the same skin friction as S21 and no undercut, so the bentonite annulus of O2, which needs the overcut, does not close it. It waits on the concept choice.
- **OB5, jerking the bailer (option B, O1).** Up to 260 N each at the end of a stroke, over the 150 N of PR-005. Fixed only if option B is chosen: a jerk lever or a third person.

## Rows that stay open

- **S2, closing force in sand.** Through the 3:1 tackle each lip closes with about 349 N against about 270 N of passive drag at full bite depth in loose saturated sand. The review found that closing starts with the grab empty, that the three sheaves lose 10 to 15 %, and that whether 1.06 times the line pull acts on each lip or on both is not settled; the margin lies between about 0.9 and 1.8. Closed by the test-pit bite trial (TRL 4) in each soil; PR-003 is at risk.
- **S3, opening by head weight.** The 14.3 kg head opens the shells when the line is slacked; whether sand lying on the lips resists it is a trial question.
- **S8, hoisting power.** The 75 W sustained figure has no source and suits leg pedalling; arm cranking is nearer 30 to 40 W, which puts the cycle near 3.7 min. A cited arm-crank figure and the timed trial decide.
- **S10a, wet brake.** At a friction coefficient of 0.25 the brake holds about 168 N m, over the 130 N m the slip clutch now lets the line apply (186 N m with the crank pin); at 0.20 it holds about 117 N m, just under, but over the 103 N m the scraper string needs; measured wet before use.
- **S14a, spooling.** The 1.91 degree fleet angle is over the 1.5 degrees of wire-rope guidance for a plain drum; the operator guides the third layer by hand.
- **S22, quick condition and loss of ground.** A fast hoist of a sealed grab can draw a transient gradient, and sand can run in under the edge without boiling; hoist slowly off the bottom and keep the inside level with the outside.
- **S5, S6, S7, E2, S21 and B4.** Restated for the decisions of 2026-10-25 (above); closed by CalRig, the test pit and the timed scraping trial.
- **OB3 (option B).** Bailer fill by spudding.
